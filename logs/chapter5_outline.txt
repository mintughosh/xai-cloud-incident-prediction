5.4 RQ3: Operational Actionability of Explanations (target: 200 words)

- Argue BGL's high actionability despite low agreement suggests SHAP's rankings, even if unstable relative to LIME, are individually recognisable to an engineer — a nuance the RQ2 metric alone would miss.

5.5 The OpenStack Sequencing Discovery (target: 180 words)
Must cover:
- Foreground the finding that 100% of the anomalous instances' triggered templates categorise as "Unknown" — none are explicit failure messages, all are routine VM lifecycle logs.
- Argue this is a genuine methodological discovery, not just a limitation: event-count feature representations (used throughout this study and much of the log-anomaly literature) are fundamentally blind to anomalies that manifest as abnormal timing/sequencing rather than abnormal event occurrence.
References: results/rq3_openstack_qualitative.txt

5.6 Limitations of the Study (target: 200 words)
Must cover:
- Benchmark datasets are static and dated (2005-2017) — no live OpenTelemetry validation was performed, a genuine gap relative to the dissertation's own title.
- SHAP/LIME were run on a 200-instance stratified sample, not the full test set, for tractability — results are representative but not exhaustive.
- The RQ3 categorisation rubric is keyword-based and manually iterated during development; while corrected for known false positives, it is not independently validated by a second annotator (a threats-to-validity point).
