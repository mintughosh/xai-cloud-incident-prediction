- Review classical ML vs deep learning approaches in AIOps literature and the accuracy/interpretability trade-off each makes.
- Note the specific gap: most AIOps papers report detection accuracy without operational-trust evaluation — set up why RQ2/RQ3 exist.

2.4 Log-Based Anomaly Detection (target: 600 words)

2.5 Explainability Methods: SHAP and LIME (target: 550 words)
Must cover:
- Theoretical basis of SHAP (Shapley values, TreeExplainer's tree-path-dependent computation) and LIME (local surrogate linear models via perturbation sampling).
- Known failure modes documented in prior literature: SHAP's additivity assumptions break under certain sklearn configurations (class-weighted models); LIME's sensitivity to perturbation sampling and discretisation choices.
- Review prior SHAP-vs-LIME agreement studies outside the log domain, to contextualise why this study's r=0.676 (HDFS) vs r=0.113 (BGL) finding is/isn't consistent with existing evidence.
References: none directly (comparative figures come later in Ch.4/5), but this section's claims are the literature basis for interpreting results/shap_lime_spearman.csv

- Review prior work on chronological vs random splitting for time-ordered operational data, and evidence that random splits inflate reported performance — direct justification for this study's Rule 1 (no random split).
